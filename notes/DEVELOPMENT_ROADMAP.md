## Milestone 6 notes
- Done: pooled traffic system (`src/traffic/`): 6 vehicle types (sedan, sports car, station wagon, delivery van, bus, semi truck) with their own speed ranges and sizes; spawning in the distance and pre-populated at start; per-car cruising speeds; car-following (slow down behind slower cars or the player); lane changes only into gaps that are clear ahead and behind (including the player); recycling once cars are far behind/ahead; collision detection against the player with damage, speed loss, shove and a crash popup/flash/shake; near-miss detection; pass counter; difficulty presets (easy/normal/hard/extreme) and `difficultyForLevel` for career scaling; traffic feeds the rear-view mirror.
- Rendering: cars are scaled sprites projected onto the road (`RoadRenderer.projectSprite`), scaled by real metres, hidden behind hill crests, fogged by distance, tinted by time of day, and flipped so each car shows the side facing the road centre.
- Verified: `npm run sim:traffic` (headless, 26 checks, all passing, included in `npm run test:sim`) and a browser drive: trucks/cars at different distances, night tint, crash popup with damage and cracked glass.
- Not done / limitations: all traffic drives the same direction as the player (the `_front` oncoming art is unused so far); sprites are the art's 3/4 rear views (no true straight-on rear view); no brake lights/headlight glow; hill-crest hiding is implemented but only lightly checked visually; no sounds (Milestone 13).

### Traffic tuning pass (after Milestone 6)
- Denser: 14 cars per 1.0 density (easy ~8, normal ~14, hard ~21, extreme ~28 target; measured averages 9.8 / 16.1 / 20.4 / 25.4 in the sim).
- Lane discipline: left lane cruises 12% faster, right lane 12% slower, so passing happens naturally.
- Overtakers: fast cars now come up from behind a moving player (visible in the mirror), not just when you are slow.
- Safer AI: emergency braking when close and traffic never drives through the car ahead (the earlier same-lane overlaps are gone: minimum gap is now positive in the sim).
- Fairer crashes: traffic collisions cost 0.6 x the closing speed in damage (was 0.9; about two thirds of before, still harsher than a lone guardrail hit at the same speed only when rear-ending fast).
- Checks: `npm run test:sim` now also covers lane bias and overtakers; all pass.

### Traffic visual fix (after Milestone 6)
Feedback: cars looked angled and did not follow the road, and the semi truck looked bad. Cause: the art was a fixed 3/4 view (and the truck at only ~26 px/m). Fix: new straight-on rear sprites at 64 px/m (about 2.4x the truck's old resolution), no flipping, drawn upright on the road. Cars still stay upright through curves (they are billboards), which matches the look of classic racers. Verified in the browser: semi truck, bus, van, sedan, sports car and wagon on a curve; all checks pass.

## Milestone 7 notes
- Done: six data-driven environments (`src/road/environments/`): Mountain Pass, Desert Highway, Coastal Highway, Forest Highway, Countryside and Night Expressway. Each defines a palette, a road layout (curves/hills/straights built with `TrackBuilder`), scenery rules and a backdrop.
- Scenery: deterministic (seeded) roadside placement with rules for spacing, distance from the road and size; wide objects are kept off the road; warning signs appear ahead of curves, plus speed/info signs and mile markers. Rendered as pooled, depth-sorted sprites scaled by real metres, hidden under hill crests, fogged by distance, and tinted by time of day (`SceneryRenderer`).
- Environmental transitions: environments can be chained into one road ("Journey"). Colours blend per segment across each boundary (60 segments each side), backdrops cross-fade, scenery thins out and the next environment's appears, and a zone banner shows the new name. Time of day is per environment (the night expressway is at night) with a player override.
- Backdrops: sky plus mountain layers (mountain, night), a mesa strip (desert), distant mountains (coastal), green hills (forest, countryside). The coastal road shows the sea on its left via a per-palette ground colour.
- Verified: `npm run sim:world` (headless, all passing, included in `npm run test:sim`) and a browser pass over all six environments plus the journey transitions at 60 FPS with ~110-180 scenery sprites.
- Not done / limitations: the `coastal_ocean_background`, `scenery_tunnel`, `road_bridge`, `scenery_mountain_*` art is unused; weather effects (rain/fog overlays) and animated sea are not wired; scenery near the road is upscaled from 16 px/m art so it looks chunky up close; guardrails are drawn on both sides even on the coast; the environment selector (B) is a test-scene feature, not yet a menu.

### Environment polish (after Milestone 7)
- Weather: storms now bring animated rain, windshield splashes, a fog bank, thicker road haze, and lightning bolts with white flashes (`WeatherRenderer`). Weather follows the time of day (storm), eases in/out and blends across zone boundaries.
- Overpasses: `road_bridge` spans the road in the mountain, forest, countryside and night environments (a `center` scenery rule); it stays solid as you pass under it.
- Coastal guardrails are on the sea side only (`barrierSide`, also respected by the collision code).
- Near objects: more clearance from the road, and very close objects dissolve instead of looming as giant blocky pixels.
- Checks: world sim extended (overpasses, one-sided barriers, weather); all sims pass. Browser-verified: storm on the coast (rain, fog, lightning, lamp on), overpass deck across the windshield on the forest road.
- Still open: tunnel and ocean art unused; sea not animated; rain/splash sheets are 7680 px wide (may need splitting for some mobile GPUs).

## Milestone 8 notes
- Done: law enforcement (`src/police/`). **Heat** builds when you exceed the posted limit by more than 20 km/h (each environment has its own limit) and from crashes (traffic hit +28, guardrail +8, near miss +2.5); it cools when you slow down. **Patrol cruisers** appear in the traffic flow (lights off) and start a pursuit if you pass them while speeding. A pursuit also starts at full heat or on demand (police challenge).
- Pursuit: cruisers spawn far behind, close the gap, sit on your tail, avoid traffic, and (levels 2+) try to get ahead and cut into your lane to block you. Every 25 s the force escalates (another cruiser, up to the level's maximum, and about 8 km/h faster). Staying close fills the **bust meter** (much faster if you stop); staying far away long enough fills the **escape meter**.
- Difficulty: four levels (Highway Patrol 195 km/h, State Troopers 228, Interceptors 258, Elite Pursuit Unit 290) with their own acceleration, arrest time, blocking chance, unit count, cash reward (500/1200/2500/5000) and fine (300/700/1500/3000).
- Scoring/results: score per second under pressure (more for speed and level) plus near-miss bonuses and an escape bonus; `PursuitResult` returns outcome, score, cash, fine and reputation change for the career milestone.
- Interface: pursuit HUD (speed-limit sign + heat bar when speeding; flashing banner with level, units, distance, arrest/escape bars; result card), red/blue siren glow on the windshield, flashing lights in the rear-view mirror, new straight-on police cruiser sprites (light bar off/red/blue/both). Busted: the car is braked to a stop automatically.
- Verified: `npm run sim:police` (headless, all passing, in `npm run test:sim`) and a browser run: forced pursuit, banner and bars, arrest with the result card, cruiser sprite with flashing bar ahead on the road.
- Not done: no helicopter (art exists), no roadblocks/spike strips, police ignore the player's lane-change tricks beyond simple blocking, no siren audio (Milestone 13), fines/rewards are not applied to money yet (Milestone 11), no pull-over steering when busted.

## Milestone 9 notes
- Done: the garage (`GarageScene`, reachable from the main menu): a **showroom** to browse and buy the six cars, and a **workshop** to upgrade and repair the active car. Money, reputation, owned cars, upgrades and damage live in a `PlayerProfile` (`src/economy/`).
- Economy: you start with the Vortex GT and $2,500. Cars cost $12,000-$95,000 (so more cars have to be earned in career mode). Seven upgrade categories x 3 levels (engine, transmission, tires, brakes, suspension, fuel tank, handling); each level costs a fraction of the car's price (6% / 10% / 16% x a category factor), so upgrades on better cars cost more. Repairs cost 0.35% of the car's price per percent of damage; with too little money you can repair part of it.
- Every upgrade changes a number the driving model uses: engine (+3.5% top speed, +7% acceleration per level), transmission (+4% acceleration, shift time 0.25 s down to ~0.115 s), tires (+0.04 grip, +3% braking), brakes (+8% braking), suspension (+7% toughness, +0.015 grip), fuel tank (+12% capacity), handling (+0.05 grip, capped at 1.2). The workshop shows the effect on six stat bars with a green preview of the selected upgrade.
- Damage is carried between drives: leaving a drive stores the car's damage, the garage shows it, and repairs cost money. Drives start with the upgraded car and its remaining damage.
- Profile storage is ready for the save system: `serializeProfile` / `deserializeProfile` (versioned, repairs bad fields, falls back to a fresh profile on corrupt data).
- Verified: `npm run sim:economy` (headless; 47 checks, all passing, in `npm run test:sim`) including measured gameplay effects of every upgrade (0-150 km/h time, top speed, stopping time, turn-in, crash damage), and a browser walk-through: browse, buy, upgrade, repair, drive (upgrade reached the physics, damage saved and repaired).
- Not done: nothing is saved between sessions yet (Milestone 14); no selling cars; no test drive of a car you do not own; money is only earned through the dev cheat (C) until career mode (Milestone 11); touch use of the garage was not tested on a device.

## Milestone 10 notes
- Done: **Arcade mode** reachable from the main menu. An arcade menu lists six challenges (Time Trial, Checkpoint Rush, Long Haul, Rush Hour, Redline, Hot Pursuit) with your best score and medal targets; pick one, choose the road with Left/Right, and start. You drive your selected (upgraded) car.
- Rules (`src/arcade/`, pure TypeScript, headless-tested): 3-second countdown with the car held on the grid, then a clock. **Time trial:** reach a finish gate 3 km away before time runs out. **Checkpoint rush:** a gate every 1,000 m adds 16 s; ends when time runs out (capped at 4 minutes). **Distance:** go as far as possible in 150 s. **Survival:** extreme traffic, survive 90 s; wrecking the car fails it; near misses count double. **High speed:** stay above 170 km/h or the speed meter drains and the run ends. **Police escape:** a pursuit is already on; lose the police within 2 minutes or you fail (busted = fail).
- Scoring: distance, speed above 100 km/h, near misses, checkpoints, time left, police escapes, minus crashes; bronze/silver/gold medals per challenge. Medals are awarded only for successful runs.
- Gates: checkpoint and finish gates are drawn on the road (generated textures) and move to the next checkpoint as you pass each one.
- HUD and flow: clock, score, objective and speed meter; countdown and popups; Esc pauses (Esc resumes, Q quits); at the end a **results screen** shows the outcome, score breakdown, medal, new-high-score / rank, with Retry and Arcade Menu.
- High scores: top 5 per challenge are stored in the player profile (and survive save/load, with corrupt data repaired).
- Developer keys (road, time, car, traffic, police, cheats) are disabled during arcade runs.
- Verified: `npm run sim:arcade` (headless; all rules, scoring, medals and high scores) and a browser pass: arcade menu, countdown, running HUD, finish gate in the distance, finishing, the results screen with a silver medal and a new high score, the Hot Pursuit challenge with the police HUD plus arcade HUD, and pausing.
- Not done / limitations: scores persist only in memory until the save milestone; the medal targets are tuned from simulated runs, not real playtests; the checkpoint, survival and high-speed challenges were verified headless and for HUD/flow but not played to the end by hand; no arcade-specific audio; no leaderboard names (entries show the car used).

## Milestone 11 notes
- Done: **Career mode** (main menu -> CAREER): a job board with 15 jobs in 5 tiers, built on the arcade rules engine (time trials, checkpoint runs, distance runs, survival, high-speed runs and police escapes). Each job has a client, a road, requirements for your car, and pay.
- Player profile: money, reputation and cleared jobs (best medal, score, times cleared) in the `PlayerProfile`; ranks by reputation: Rookie, Courier (50), Driver (150), Ace (300), Legend (600).
- Reputation unlocks content: jobs appear at tier reputation levels; roads open at Forest 50, Coastal 90, Desert 120, Night Expressway 150 (Mountain and Countryside from the start).
- Meaningful reasons to buy and upgrade cars: jobs require top speed, acceleration, handling, fuel capacity or toughness, checked against your car **with its upgrades**. Examples: Overnight Freight needs a 55 L tank (the starter has 50 L; a fuel upgrade or the Thunder V8 qualifies); Desert Dash needs acceleration 24 (Thunder, Silverline, Phantom); the Elite Pursuit needs 320 km/h (only a Phantom X with an engine upgrade); the Grand Tour needs speed, handling and toughness together (only a fully upgraded Silverline Turbo). Each tier-3+ job is out of reach of the starter car without an upgrade or a new car.
- Pay: bronze/silver/gold = x1 / x1.3 / x1.7 of the job's pay, +25% for a first clear, 60% on replays (so grinding is slow). Reputation: tier x 12 (+4 silver, +8 gold) on a first clear, a quarter on replays. Being busted in a police job costs a fine (half the police level's fine); a car at 75% damage or more cannot start a job (repair first).
- Flow: job board (requirement checklist and pay per medal) -> arcade-style run with countdown/HUD -> results screen with payout, reputation, rank-ups and unlocks -> back to the board; Garage reachable from the board with G.
- Verified: `npm run sim:career` (headless; job data validity, requirement rules, ranks/unlocks, payout maths, completion rules, pacing, save/load) and a browser run: job board with locked/available/cleared states, an actual job (pizza, gold: +$850, +20 rep), the results payout screen, and board states at higher reputation. In the hidden-pane test browser the game loop was stepped manually.
- Not done / limitations: job balance (times, medal scores, pay) comes from simulation, not playtests; no story or dialogue beyond client lines; no job-specific cutscenes; progress is in memory until the save milestone; career money and repairs are shared with the garage (no separate career garage).

## Milestone 12 notes
- Done: **touch controls** (`src/controls/Touch*.ts`), shown automatically on touch devices (or forced On/Off in settings) and hidden on desktop until the first touch. Layout is in 960x540 game space: steering on the left (two big buttons **or** a virtual steering wheel; tap the small WHEEL/BUTTONS toggle), brake and an analogue gas pedal on the right (press nearer the bottom for more throttle), SHIFT +/- and AUTO/MANUAL buttons, and a pause button. Controls are 118+ px (game space) so they stay thumb-sized on a phone.
- Multitouch: every finger owns the control it landed on until it lifts, so gas + steer + brake + gear taps work together; sliding off a button does not drop it. The wheel springs back to centre on release and locks at 60 degrees of finger rotation.
- Pause on touch shows tappable RESUME / QUIT buttons. A portrait phone shows a "rotate to landscape" screen (CSS only).
- **Adaptive quality** (`QualityManager`): High / Medium / Low change draw distance, scenery pool and weather detail; Auto drops a level after 2 s under 45 fps and raises one after 12 s above 57 fps (with cooldowns). Phones start at Medium. Settings (`src/save/Settings.ts`: steer mode, touch controls, opacity, quality) are read defensively from localStorage.
- Mobile texture limit: the rain/splash sheets were 7680 px wide (bug 029); `scripts/reslice-sheets.mjs` re-gridded them to 4x2 (3840x1080). `assets:verify` now fails any PNG over 4096 px.
- Verified: `npm run sim:touch` (layout overlap/size checks, pedals, multitouch, one-shot buttons, wheel maths, input merge, quality up/down, settings parsing, texture limits) plus all earlier sims; build OK. Browser run at 844x390 with real `TouchEvent`s: gas + steer + brake simultaneously, lifting one finger leaves the others, wheel mode (30 degrees = half lock, springs back), mode toggle, portrait overlay at 390x844.
- Not done / limitations: **not tested on a real phone or tablet** (only an emulated viewport and synthetic touches), so feel, thumb reach and real frame rates are unverified; menu/garage/arcade/career screens use the existing tap-to-select buttons without extra touch tuning; no haptics; no tilt steering; no settings screen yet to change the touch options (the values exist in `Settings.ts`; the screen arrives with the save work in Milestone 14).

## Milestone 13 notes
- Done: a complete **procedural audio engine** (`src/audio/`). Every sound is synthesised in the browser with WebAudio, so there are **no audio files** and nothing to license; all music is original and written as data.
- Engines: six distinct engine voices, one per car (V6, inline-6, flat-6, V8, turbo-4, V12), built from sawtooth/square/sub oscillators + intake noise through a low-pass filter. Pitch follows the real firing rate from RPM and cylinder count, brightness and loudness follow throttle and revs, the rev limiter stutters, the V8 idles lumpy, the turbo car whistles under boost and blows off on shifts, and the engine ducks during gear changes and dies when the tank is empty.
- Driving sounds: tyre squeal (skidding and hard braking at speed), wind (grows with speed), rumble on the shoulder/grass (different roughness), rain ambience in storms, damage rattle, gear-shift clunk, guardrail scrape, crash (scaled to impact speed, panned to the side hit), near-miss and pass-by whooshes (panned), engine sputter, and a low drone from traffic cars alongside.
- Police: a two-tone siren from the nearest chase car with distance falloff (audible to ~600 m), stereo pan and Doppler pitch shift; busted and escaped stingers.
- Music: three original 80s-style tracks (`MusicData.ts`): **Neon Dawn** (menu, 92 bpm), **Midnight Run** (arcade/career runs, 138 bpm), **Hot Pursuit** (switches in during a police chase, 156 bpm). A look-ahead scheduler plays them and crossfades between tracks; music is ducked to half while driving so engine and sirens stay clear. The dev Road Test has no music.
- UI sounds: menu move/select/back/error, cash register on purchases, countdown beeps and GO, checkpoint chime, finish fanfare.
- Controls: **X** toggles mute; a SOUND ON/OFF button is on the main menu and in the arcade pause menu (so it works on touch). Master/music/effects volumes and mute are stored in settings (`Settings.ts`). Audio unlocks on the first click, key or touch (browser rule), pauses when the tab is hidden, and silences during pause.
- Verified: `npm run sim:audio` (engine profiles and pitch separation for all six cars, mixing rules and ranges, siren/Doppler maths, driving-state to sound mapping, music data validity and loop timing, settings) plus all earlier sims; build OK. In the browser the real voices, effects and music were rendered through an OfflineAudioContext and measured (no NaN/clipping; idle to redline about 5x louder; pitch exactly doubles when revs double); in the live game the master output rose from 0.027 RMS at idle to 0.10 at 159 km/h, menu music played and muted to silence and back, and shifts, scrapes, crashes, pass-bys, countdown beeps, GO and the pursuit music switch all fired.
- Not done / limitations: **nobody has listened to it yet**: levels and taste were checked with measurements, not ears or real speakers/headphones, so balance and character need a human ear; iOS Safari (silent switch, audio unlock) is untested; engines are synthesised approximations, not recordings; no separate pause-menu music; no volume sliders yet (values exist in settings, UI comes with the settings screen); pass-by pan is random because traffic passes do not report a side; helicopters, tunnels/echo and per-surface tyre-roll sounds are not modelled.

## Milestone 14 notes
- Done: a **save system** (`src/save/`). It stores the profile (money, reputation, owned cars, upgrades, damage, arcade high scores, completed career jobs; unlocked roads follow from reputation), all settings, and the keyboard bindings. Saving is automatic: on every change within about 2 seconds, right after purchases, repairs and runs, and when the page is hidden or closed. The main menu shows your money, reputation and rank.
- File format: one versioned text file (`SaveFile.ts`): `{format, version, savedAt, checksum, data}`. A **checksum** catches truncated or hand-edited files; a **version** number plus a migration table (`MIGRATIONS`, one step per format change) upgrades old saves; a save from a *newer* game is loaded as well as possible and an untouched copy is kept before it is overwritten.
- Safety: reading never throws. Every value goes through the defensive parsers (clamped numbers, unknown cars/keys dropped, owned-car rules). Writes keep the **last good save as a backup**, verify what they wrote, and restore the old file if a write fails (for example storage full), so a bad write cannot destroy a good save. If the main save is damaged the game restores the backup, keeps the damaged text aside (`.corrupt`) and shows a notice on the main menu; if nothing is usable it starts fresh and says so.
- Modular for cloud saving: `SaveBackend` is a tiny interface (read/write/remove); the game uses `localStorage`, with an in-memory fallback if the browser blocks storage (the Settings screen warns when that happens). A cloud backend, or a wrapper that also uploads, can be added without touching game code.
- **Settings screen** (main menu > SETTINGS): master/music/effects volume, sound on/off, touch controls (auto/on/off), touch steering (buttons/wheel), touch opacity, graphics quality, **key rebinding** (press the new key; a key taken from another action is removed there; X and a few browser keys are reserved), **export / import** of the save text (copy to move progress to another device), and a two-step **reset progress** (settings and keys are kept; the old progress stays in the backup).
- Verified: `npm run sim:save` (round trip of every saved field, 19 kinds of unreadable file, sanitising of bad values, migrations incl. failing ones, newer versions, every recovery path, failed writes, import/export, reset, a 600-case random-corruption fuzz test, key binding rules) plus all earlier sims; build OK. In the browser against real localStorage: a fresh start writes a save; buying a car and upgrades autosaved and survived a page reload; truncating the stored save restored the backup with a notice and kept the damaged copy; volume, graphics quality and a rebound key persisted across a reload and the rebound key drove the car; car damage persisted after a drive; reset progress worked and kept settings.
- Not done / limitations: one save slot only; browser data being cleared (or private browsing) loses progress, and there is no cloud save yet; saves are not encrypted (the checksum only detects accidents, so players can still edit values); the dev money cheat (C in the garage) is saved like any other money until it is removed in Milestone 15; the settings screen is keyboard- and tap-friendly but untested on a real touch device, and gamepads cannot rebind or navigate it; export/import uses the clipboard or a browser prompt, untested on phones.

## Milestone 15 notes (bug fixes and optimization pass)
- Bugs fixed: the developer money cheat (C) and the Road Test menu entry now exist only in development builds (035/053); gear down defaults to **Z** instead of Ctrl, because Ctrl+W (accelerate) closed the browser tab (010); pass-by whooshes pan to the side the car was actually passed on (048); stale entries in the bug log were updated (001, 005, 032, 034); the Settings screen has a **Fullscreen** option (not offered on iPhones, which cannot do it).
- Measured first: a typical drive costs about **2.1 ms per frame** on the desktop test browser (budget 16.7 ms), split roughly 0.5 ms game logic and 1.5 ms drawing. Assets total only 1.6 MB.
- Optimizations (all measured):
  - **Police HUD**: it cleared and re-set its text every frame, forcing Phaser to re-draw the text and re-upload it. It was the most expensive single item (**0.28 ms per frame**, more than the whole road renderer); now **0.006 ms** (speeding) / 0.016 ms (chase). The arcade HUD had the same pattern with its timer colour and is fixed, and a shared helper (`UI/textUtil.ts`, `setTextCached`) updates text only when it changes.
  - **Road renderer**: the five shoulder/rumble/road quads per segment became three nested quads (same picture), cutting graphics calls from about 2,010 to **1,640 per frame (-19%)**; far-away rumble strips, edge lines and lane dashes that are under a pixel wide are skipped by a level-of-detail setting tied to graphics quality (medium/low cull more; high is unchanged); per-frame object and closure allocation was removed. `npm run bench` measures allocation (about 1 KB per frame for the road, under 3 KB for traffic and police) and graphics calls per quality level.
  - Small allocation fixes: police unit counting without array copies, traffic events object reused, no spread/map in the scene update.

## Close-up scenery and screen scaling
- **Close-up scenery (fixed).** Scenery art is 16 pixels per metre, so an object passing the car is drawn at 3-5 screen pixels per art pixel: big squares next to the 1:1 cockpit art, and at in-between scales (like 1.75x) the pixels alternated between 1 and 2 px wide. Now each object picks a texture density so one art pixel is about one screen pixel: plain art below 1.3x, a smoothed 2x version up to 2.6x, a smoothed 4x version above. The smoothed versions use the pixel-art **EPX / Scale2x** algorithm (no blur, no new colours, only corners and diagonal steps change), built once per road in the background (a few ms per frame; the texture uploads cost 10-15 ms on one frame each right after a road loads) and only on High quality (7-19 MB of textures per road, 50 MB for the chained Journey). Verified: a side-by-side of the same tree plain and smoothed at 3x and 1.75x, the sprites switching texture in the running game, a unit test of the algorithm (including that slice-by-slice scaling equals all-at-once) and the memory budget (`npm run sim:render`).
- **How big is the scaling issue?** The game draws a fixed 960x540 picture and the browser scales it up with nearest-neighbour. That is perfect when the scale is a whole number of *device* pixels and uneven otherwise (some picture pixels end up 1 device pixel wide, some 2, or 2 and 3), which shows as thin lines of uneven thickness and slight shimmer while things move. Measured scale (device pixels per game pixel) for common screens: 1920x1080 monitor 2.00 (perfect); 125%-scaled 1536x864 laptop 2.00 (perfect); 4K at 200% 4.00 (perfect); retina MacBook 1440x900 3.00 (perfect); Android phones (915x412 at 2.625, 851x393 at 2.75) about 2.00 (perfect); iPhone landscape 844x390 2.17 (minor); 2560x1440 monitor 2.67 (noticeable but subtle); **1366x768 laptop 1.42, 1280x720 window 1.33 and 1600x900 window 1.67 are the bad cases** (columns alternate 1 and 2 pixels wide). So it is a **minor-to-moderate cosmetic issue that affects a specific band of screens** (small laptops and windowed play), not a functional problem; text is smooth-filtered and less affected. Not changed: snapping to a whole number only helps sizes above 2x (snapping 1.33 down to 1x would shrink the picture by 25%), so a real fix for the bad band would need a higher-resolution render target or a "sharp bilinear" shader, which is a bigger change.

## Even-pixel screen scaling (fixes the "bad band")
- **Problem.** The 960x540 picture was scaled to the screen with nearest-neighbour. At a fractional scale of device pixels (windows and laptops between 1x and 2x, such as 1366x768, 1280x720 and 1600x900, and 1440p monitors at 2.67x) game pixels came out 1 and 2 device pixels wide in an uneven pattern. Phaser 4 has no render-resolution option, and snapping to a whole number would shrink the picture (1.33x down to 1x loses 25%).
- **Fix: sharp bilinear scaling** (`src/core/SharpScaler.ts`, rules in `sharpPlan.ts`). After every rendered frame: (1) the game canvas is copied into a buffer enlarged by a whole number (2x, or 3x for 2-3x screens) with crisp nearest-neighbour pixels, then (2) that buffer is smoothly shrunk into a canvas of exactly the screen's device-pixel size, shown over the game. Every game pixel then covers the same area, with only a soft edge between neighbours. The Phaser canvas stays underneath and keeps receiving all input (the overlay ignores the pointer), so mouse and touch mapping is unchanged.
- **When it runs.** Only for fractional scales from 1x up to (not including) 3x. It switches itself off at whole scales (1080p, 4K, a 125%-scaled 1536x864 laptop, retina MacBooks), at 3x and above (uneven pixels are too small to notice), and on touch screens from 2x up (phones already sit near a whole number, and the extra copy costs battery). It follows window resizes, fullscreen and zoom changes. Settings > **PIXEL SCALING** (EVEN / ORIGINAL) turns it off; the choice is saved.
- **Measured** in the browser pane at a 1.43 scale (like a 1366x768 laptop), using alternating 1-pixel stripes: with plain scaling stripes alternate 1 and 2 device pixels wide (white mass per stripe varies 1.0 to 2.0, spread 34.6%); with even pixels it varies 1.19 to 1.60 (spread 10.7%), about 3x more even. A 3x or 4x intermediate buffer made no real difference (9.1%, 10.5%), so 2x is used. The overlay matched the canvas to the device pixel at 1.43x and 1.67x, switched itself off at exactly 2.0x, kept the pointer on the game canvas (a click opened the Garage and the pointer read the right game coordinate), and added about 0.1 ms of CPU time per frame.
- **Limits.** The residual 10% is inherent to 1-pixel detail at fractional scales; edges are very slightly soft (that is what "even" costs; ORIGINAL restores hard but uneven pixels). The GPU cost of the two copies and the visual result on real displays were not measured (the pane's screenshots show only the game canvas, not the overlay, so the check was numeric).

## Damage and cracked windshield (fix)
- Cause: guardrail contact applied damage every frame while the car stayed against the rail, and damage is saved between drives, so a single mistake left the windshield cracked from then on (cracks started at 25% damage and repairs cost more than the starting money). Fixed by counting a scrape once per half second at a lower rate, raising the crack thresholds to 40 / 65 / 85% and clearing damage under 15% for free after each drive (`MINOR_DAMAGE` in `economy/Profile.ts`, `BARRIER_IMPACT_FACTOR` in `DrivingModel.ts`, `CRACK_STAGES` in `UI/CockpitView.ts`). New tests in `sim:driving` (a 2-second scrape stays under 20% and counts as a few incidents) and `sim:economy` (the free-dent rule); the running game was checked at 0-100% damage: clean glass up to 39%, cracks from 40%.
- Not changed: traffic hits (they already cost less per km/h), repair prices, and the guardrail slow-down. Playtesting should confirm the new numbers feel fair.

## Wheels vs gauges (measured fix)
- I had only judged the wheels by eye. `scripts/wheel-gauge-overlap.mjs` now renders each car's rim and spoke frames at every steering angle the game can show and counts how much of every gauge they cover. First measurement: rim alone 5-21% of the tachometer, spokes up to 45% at full lock. Why: two big gauges side by side (x 205 and 275, radius 30, tops at y 184) cannot sit inside a nearly round wheel whose top edge must stay below the gear display (it ended at y = 169).
